\subsection{Zhang's method}\label{subsec:zhangsmethod}

The goal of Zhang's method~\citep{zhang} is to precisely find the position of multiple cameras in space ($R$ and $t$).
To achieve this, all that is required is that all cameras have a checkerboard with known dimensions in their frame.
Importantly, nothing must be known in advance about the extrinsics ($R$, $t$) or the intrinsics $K$ of the cameras.

The first step is to locate the checkerboard in the frame of every camera and compute the homography that maps the
(known) 3D-coordinates of the feature points on the 2D image.
By convention, Zhang chooses to put the checkerboard in the $XY$ plane ($Z=0$).
Since the precise dimensions of the checkerboard are known, we know with full precision the position $(X,Y,0)$ in 3D
space of every feature point (the intersection of 4 squares) on the checkerboard.

We have:
\begin{align*}
    \left[\begin{array}{c}
              x \\ y \\ 1
    \end{array}\right] &= K\left[
    \begin{array}{c c c c}
        \textbf{r}_1 & \textbf{r}_2 & \textbf{r}_3 & \textbf{t}
    \end{array}\right]\cdot
    \left[\begin{array}{c}
              X \\ Y \\ 0 \\ 1
    \end{array}\right]\\
    &=K\left[
    \begin{array}{c c c}
        \textbf{r}_1 & \textbf{r}_2 & \textbf{t}
    \end{array}\right]\cdot
    \left[\begin{array}{c}
              X \\ Y \\ 1
    \end{array}\right]
\end{align*}

The homography:
\[H=\left[\begin{array}{c c c}
        \textbf{h}_1 & \textbf{h}_2 & \textbf{h}_3
    \end{array}\right]=K\left[\begin{array}{c c c}
        \textbf{r}_1 & \textbf{r}_2 & \textbf{t}
    \end{array}\right]\]
can be estimated using the regular techniques to find homographies (section~\ref{subsec:homography}).

If we let:
\[\textbf{v}_{ij}=\left[\begin{array}{c c c c c c}
         h_{i1} h_{j1} & h_{i1}h_{j2}+h_{i2}h_{j1} & h_{i2}h_{j2} & h_{i3}h_{j1}+h_{i1}h_{j3} & h_{i3}h_{j2}+h_{i2}h_{j3}
                            & h_{i3}h_{j3}
\end{array}\right]^t\]

Then, we can retrieve a 6-dimensional vector $\textbf{b}$ by solving the equation:
\[\left[\begin{array}{c}
            \textbf{v}^t_{12}\\
            (\textbf{v}_{11}-\textbf{v}_{22})^t
\end{array}\right]\textbf{b}=\textbf{0}\]
Note that each estimated $H$ gives two new equations but $\textbf{b}$ contains 6 unknowns.
Hence, we need at least 3 different images of the checkerboard (we assume that all cameras have the same $K$ matrix, so
simply one image per camera).

Using the vector $\textbf{b}$, we can extract the intrinsics of the camera(s) $K$ (see appendix B of~\citep{zhang}).
With the estimated $K$ and the homography $H$ (that are different for all cameras), it is possible to approximate the
rotation $R$ and the translation $t$.

These initial $R_i$ and $t_i$ can be computed for all cameras $i$ ($k_1,k_2$ are all set to 0 initially, \textit{QZ.1}).
See section 3.1 of~\citep{zhang} for a more in-depth explanation.

We have $n$ cameras and $m$ known 3D points $\textit{M}_j$ on the checkerboard.
For all $n$ cameras, we know the 2D-coordinates of the $j$th checkerboard point projected onto the image plane
$\textbf{m}_{ij}$ (we assume here that the radial distortion is not removed from the image).
Let $\hat{\textbf{m}}(K,R_i,t_i,\textit{M}_j)$ be a function that computes the projection of the 3D point $\textit{M}_j$
onto the image plane of a camera with extrinsics $(R_i,t_i)$ and intrinsics $K$.
Then, the energy that is minimized to optimize $K,R_i$ and $t_i$ is (equation $10$ in~\citep{zhang}):

\[\sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{m} \left\|\textbf{m}_{ij}-\hat{\textbf{m}}(K,R_i,t_i,\textit{M}_j)\right\|_2^2\]

Remarkably, the above equation is exactly what is done in SfM except for the fact that SfM also optimizes for the 3D
position of the feature points (see page 51 of the syllabus or section~\ref{subsec:colmap}, \textit{QZ.2}).

To take into account the radial distortion (see section~\ref{subsec:radialdistortion}), we can define a new function
$\breve{\textbf{m}}(K,k_1,k_2,R_i,t_i,\textit{M}_j)$ that computes the projection of the 3D point onto the image plane but
then applies the radial distortion associated with the distortion parameters $(k_1,k_2)$.
Then, the new energy function to minimize becomes (equation 14 in~\citep{zhang}):
\[\sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{m} \left\|\textbf{m}_{ij}-\breve{\textbf{m}}(K,k_1,k_2,R_i,t_i,\textit{M}_j)\right\|_2^2\]

The exact same idea can be performed in SfM\@.

Zhang also proposes an alternative way of estimating $k_1$ and $k_2$ by alternating the optimization of the intrinsics
and extrinsincs, then the optimization of $k_1$ and $k_2$ until convergence.
The author states that this method is in practice slower than simply optimizing $k_1$ and $k_2$ at the same time as the
other parameters (see section 3.3 of~\citep{zhang}).
